How this connects to BehaviorTrace

During training, participant-confirmed activity labels are aligned with time windows. BehaviorTrace extracts features such as mean, standard deviation, minimum, maximum, energy, sample count and timing information across wearable channels. A random forest learns relationships between those features and the labels.

At prediction time, the pipeline selects a new window and computes the same features in the same order. Each trained tree follows feature thresholds to a leaf; their outputs are combined into a predicted activity. The forest does not choose the window itself—the preprocessing pipeline does.

My toy activity project predicted running, walking, sitting and standing. This interactive illustration uses a separate five-tree forest trained on synthetic data; BehaviorTrace’s training script configures 500 trees. Moving across an activity boundary also shows why a window can mix states.

A neural network is an alternative model, not a stage after the random forest. Depending on its architecture, it could learn from engineered features or the sampled sequence itself. Its weights are learned with backpropagation and an optimiser such as gradient descent—the idea illustrated in the header. This demo runs the random-forest path.

Synthetic signals in arbitrary units, deliberately separated for illustration. Real activity or emotional-state prediction needs appropriate sensors, labels, and evaluation on held-out participants or sessions. About random forests
